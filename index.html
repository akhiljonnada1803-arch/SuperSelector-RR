Methodology used

Pressure shortlist definitions, calculations and limits
01 · Scope

What was analyzed

Men’s IPL and T20 International cricket, 2022–2026. The report’s source timestamps run from 23 January 2022 through 1 November 2026. The pressure shortlist tabs reproduce the names and figures in the supplied report.

02 · Pressure

Which deliveries count

A delivery qualifies if it occurs in overs 16–20, or when four or more wickets have already fallen. Six or more wickets is noted as a severe collapse. A required run rate of 9.5 or above is not a separate trigger in this version.

03 · Sample cutoffs

Who qualifies

Batters: at least 150 pressure balls faced. Bowlers: at least 400 legal pressure balls bowled. Phil Salt (139 balls), Mitchell Starc (253), Jamie Overton (282) and Abrar Ahmed (341) remain as explicitly flagged scouting or impact exceptions.

04 · Batting

How batting figures are calculated

Strike rate = pressure runs ÷ pressure balls × 100. Batting average = pressure runs ÷ dismissals. Missing values stay blank. The shortlist order is for presentation only; no composite score or definitive ranking is used.

05 · Bowling

How bowling figures are calculated

Bowling strike rate = legal pressure balls ÷ wickets. Bowling average = estimated runs conceded ÷ wickets. Economy is estimated as 6 × bowling average ÷ bowling strike rate; treat it as indicative, not an official figure.

06 · Data limits

What the figures cannot tell us

The source’s extras field is inconsistent, and the report discloses reconciliation differences: 200,961 Stage 3 deliveries versus 200,999 raw deliveries; 867 analyzed matches versus 883 metadata matches. The Overview recalculates player metrics from raw ball data, so it may not exactly match report shortlist figures.

07 · Separate dashboard measure

Overview quality percentile

The Overview’s within-role quality percentile is separate from the report shortlists. It averages available within-role percentile ranks (0–100) for batting runs, average and strike rate, and bowling wickets and economy. Components require at least 60 recorded balls. It is a comparison aid, not a forecast, and it does not change the pressure shortlist order.

What the batter shortlist shows

The report keeps different pressure profiles instead of ranking everyone by one number:

  • High scoring impact: Suryakumar Yadav struck at 196.99 from 531 pressure balls; Naman Dhir struck at 192.39 from 289. Phil Salt struck at 192.09, but his 139 balls are below the 150-ball cutoff.
  • Large-sample accumulation: Tim David scored 1,765 from 986 pressure balls. Tilak Varma faced the largest listed sample (993 balls), with the highest listed batting average (38.71).
  • Average and survival: David Miller averaged 37.53 from 810 balls; Virat Kohli averaged 35.64 from 289; Shreyas Iyer’s dot-ball rate was 21.76% from 432 balls.
  • Scouting exception: Phil Salt is compared with Rajat Patidar. Patidar has the larger qualifying sample and slightly higher strike rate (193.58 vs 192.09); Salt has the higher average (26.70 vs 17.24) and lower dot-ball rate (22.30% vs 26.20%).

What the bowler shortlist shows

The report weighs wicket-taking against run containment, and treats economy as estimated:

  • Volume and wickets: Arshdeep Singh took 95 wickets from 1,305 legal pressure balls (bowling strike rate 13.74), with estimated economy 9.44.
  • Wicket-taking plus control: Rashid Khan took 84 wickets from 1,158 balls, estimated economy 7.65; Varun Chakravarthy took 62 from 875, estimated economy 7.15.
  • Run containment: Sunil Narine’s estimated economy is 6.14 from 571 balls; Abrar Ahmed’s is 5.60 from 341 balls, below the 400-ball cutoff.
  • Trade-off and exceptions: Mitchell Starc has a short bowling strike rate (9.37) but the highest listed estimated economy (9.94) on 253 balls. Jamie Overton (282 balls) is also below the cutoff, despite a 7.64 estimated economy.

How to interpret the shortlists

The report describes analytical profiles, not predictions or selection recommendations. A batter’s high strike rate, high average, or large sample indicates a different strength. For bowlers, wickets and economy must be read together. The report does not assign specialist roles or claim that the ordered names are ranked.

Several batter boundary and dot-ball measures are unavailable. Exact opposition quality, pitch, venue, match importance, win probability, match state and phase-specific performance are not fully captured. Required run rate is not a separate pressure trigger.

Source reconciliation

The report records 200,961 deliveries in its Stage 3 pressure dataset and 200,999 in the raw ball file, a 38-delivery difference. It analyzed 867 matches, while the match metadata contains 883 matches, a 16-match difference. These differences remain unresolved. The supplied extras column is inconsistent, and the report labels bowling economy as estimated rather than verified.

08 · The cricketer’s lens

Data supported the picks; it did not make them alone.

As a cricketer, I brought observations from watching live matches: which players absorbed pressure, stayed composed and delivered when needed. The dataset records outcomes, but cannot fully capture the pressure a player felt, decision-making, awareness of the game or the moment’s context.

My selection lens also considered consistency across performances, game awareness and adaptability, and each player’s role and impact. These are scouting judgments informed by your match observations; they are not variables measured by the CSVs and should not be presented as statistical findings.

How to read the board

Definitions and data limitations
Metrics, not mystery scores
  • Batting average = runs ÷ recorded dismissals. Blank when no dismissal is recorded.
  • Strike rate = runs ÷ balls faced × 100. Wides excluded from balls faced.
  • Economy = recorded runs ÷ (bowling balls ÷ 6). The supplied extras1 field is inconsistent (it is 6 on ordinary deliveries), so extras are excluded and economy is an estimate.
  • Quality percentile is a within-role rank (0–100) built from percentile ranks of runs, average, strike rate and wickets/economy. Batting and bowling inputs require at least 60 recorded balls. It is a comparison aid, not a forecast.
  • Role labels are inferred from recorded batting positions and bowling workload. Sample size varies; check balls, matches and dismissals before comparing.
  • RR history flags players listed for Rajasthan Royals in the mapping data. It does not imply current contract or recruitment recommendation. The pressure shortlist and displayed figures follow Report.pdf, which analyzes IPL and T20I together for 2022–2026. Its pressure definition is death overs 16–20 or four or more wickets lost. The batter qualification is 150 pressure balls; the bowler qualification is 400 legal pressure balls. Phil Salt, Mitchell Starc, Jamie Overton and Abrar Ahmed are retained below threshold as scouting or impact exceptions. The report order is for presentation, not ranking, and no composite score is used.
